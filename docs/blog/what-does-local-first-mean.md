We want AppWeaver to be a place where programmers can build applications and reach users through an open platform.

AppWeaver itself is not trying to become a new decentralized protocol. I don’t think it needs to be one.

Why do we want decentralization in the first place? Usually, because we don’t want our work, data, and relationships to depend on a tech company’s incentives. The network you build over years — by investing your time, energy, and trust — becomes part of your data. On traditional platforms, migrating that part of your data is almost impossible.

There is a saying: if you do not pay for the product, you are the product. That is sometimes true. But paying does not automatically protect you either. A paid product can still lock you in, extract your data, or shape your behavior.

AppWeaver approaches this problem from a different direction: local-first and self-hosted software.

Your data is yours. The software you run is open-source. You can install plugins into your own workspace, create new ones, and change the core code. The whole system is under your control.

You can choose to use a local AI model, or any cloud AI provider supported by OpenCode. Privacy-preserving cloud models may be useful, but if privacy is your priority, the strongest option is a local model that does not send your data outside your network.

With this philosophy, AppWeaver is not a protocol first. It is a place — a platform — that provides infrastructure for AI-powered apps you choose to run. It includes a Cashu wallet, Nostr-based identity, selectable AI backends, and a plugin manager for installing apps.

That extendable architecture lets you install plugins, connect different AI backends, and shape the workspace around your own needs.

Plugins are permissionless. I cannot restrict what gets published to relays I do not control, and that is by design. The only thing I control is the official Git repository where AppWeaver and official plugins are published. But anyone can fork them, change them, and redistribute their own version of them.

That is what local-first means for AppWeaver: not just owning your data, but owning the environment where your tools run.
